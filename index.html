Essential Defense Knowledge

Why Digital Safety Is Crucial

Cyber threats are no longer confined to military servers and Fortune 500 banks. Students, faculty, and daily internet users have become prime targets for automated threat actors.

Higher Education & Students

Campus Vulnerabilities

Colleges and universities operate open, collaborative networks where thousands of personal devices connect simultaneously. Students often balance academic stress, loan applications, and job searches, making them uniquely susceptible to targeted exploits:

  • Tuition & Financial Aid Phishing: Spoofed bursar notices demanding urgent bank verification for fake fee refunds or scholarship payments.
  • Open Campus Wi-Fi Sniffing: Connecting without encryption exposes learning management portal credentials and research files.
  • Credential Reuse Risk: Using identical passwords across college portals, social accounts, and gaming platforms creates cascading breach risks.

Everyday Internet Citizens

Consumer Protection

From mobile banking and social media to smart home IoT devices and cloud storage, modern life is fundamentally digital. A single compromised account can trigger catastrophic financial loss or permanent identity theft:

  • Synthetic Identity Theft: Criminals harvest Social Security numbers and date of birth to open fraudulent lines of credit and drain savings.
  • Social Engineering & Impersonation: Scammers clone voices or spoof family member profiles on messaging apps requesting urgent bail or gift cards.
  • Ransomware & Cloud Extortion: Personal family photos, tax records, and private documents encrypted until high cryptocurrency ransoms are paid.
Threat Taxonomy

Core Cyber Threat Vectors

Understand the anatomy, psychological triggers, and technical mechanisms behind the most pervasive digital attack vectors.

🎣
Severity: Critical

Phishing & Spear-Phishing

Fraudulent communications designed to masquerade as reputable entities (banks, colleges, courier services) to harvest sensitive credentials.

⚠️ Warning Signs:
  • Artificial deadlines & panic ("Act within 24 hours")
  • Mismatched domain suffixes (.top, .xyz, typosquatting)
  • Requests for sensitive PINs or passwords via email
🛡️ Defense: Domain verification & MFA
🦠
Severity: High

Malware & Keyloggers

Malicious software including Trojans, rootkits, and keystroke loggers that covertly exfiltrate personal data, webcam feeds, and login sessions.

⚠️ Warning Signs:
  • Unusual computer slowdowns & high background CPU usage
  • Unexpected pop-ups and redirected search engine results
  • Double file extensions (e.g., invoice.pdf.exe)
🛡️ Defense: Endpoint EDR & verified downloads
🔒
Severity: Severe

Ransomware Extortion

Cryptoviral extortion where victim files and databases are encrypted with military-grade ciphers until a cryptocurrency ransom is transferred.

⚠️ Warning Signs:
  • Files suddenly inaccessible with appended random extensions
  • Desktop wallpaper replaced with extortion countdown instructions
  • Disabled Windows Volume Shadow Copies & recovery tools
🛡️ Defense: Immutable 3-2-1 offline backups
🔑
Severity: High

Credential Stuffing & Brute Force

Automated botnets testing millions of username/password combinations leaked in past historical data breaches across multiple web services.

⚠️ Warning Signs:
  • Unexpected 2FA security prompt notifications on your phone
  • Account lockout alerts due to excessive failed attempts
  • Unrecognized device logins in account security activity
🛡️ Defense: Unique passphrases & password managers
🎭
Severity: Critical

Social Engineering & Pretexting

Manipulating human psychology rather than software vulnerabilities to trick individuals into divulging security badges, OTPs, or financial wires.

⚠️ Warning Signs:
  • Callers posing as IT demanding remote desktop access
  • Urgent requests to bypass standard institutional approval rules
  • Demands for untraceable gift cards or peer-to-peer transfers
🛡️ Defense: Out-of-band verification via phone/in-person
🪪
Severity: Severe

Identity Theft & Data Breaches

Acquiring personally identifiable information (PII) to assume another individual's persona, obtain fraudulent loans, or commit tax fraud.

⚠️ Warning Signs:
  • Unexplained hard inquiries on your credit monitoring report
  • Bills or statements arriving for credit cards you never opened
  • IRS rejections stating duplicate tax returns already filed
🛡️ Defense: Credit bureau freeze & breach monitoring
